\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:ws-intro}

An agent that calls tools will get some of those calls wrong. What happens next
is decided by the harness rather than the model: it appends the failed call to
the transcript, appends whatever the runtime printed, and asks the model to
continue. ReAct \citep{yao2023react} introduced this pattern and essentially
every agent framework since has kept it, including those built for small
open-weight models. The premise is that the error message is corrective
information, produced by ground truth rather than by a critic.

We measure whether it is, and it is not. Fix a decision point: a context
$\ctxpre$, an action $\afail$ that fails there, and the observation
$o^{\times}$ the runtime returns. Define the \emph{corrective gain} as the
change in the log-probability of writing $\afail$ again once the failure has
been recorded. Across \NumModelsToolshed\ instruction-tuned checkpoints
(\SmallestModel--\LargestModel, \NumFamilies\ families) and two environments,
the gain is \emph{negative}: recording the failure makes the failed call
\emph{more} likely, by \MeanG\ nats, or about \GPerTokenTool\ nats per action
token. The effect is not an average over a few dramatic items; it holds on
\FracNegTool\ or more of individual items.

The reason matters more than the size, because it decides which fixes can
work. Appending $(\afail, o^{\times})$ does two things at once: it places the
failed call's token sequence where the model's copying machinery can reach it
\citep{olsson2022induction,xu2022breaktheloop}, and it asserts that the call
failed. Scoring the same action against a \emph{valence-free} observation, one
that acknowledges the call without saying how it went, separates them. The
surface-form term accounts for \CopyShare\ of the effect. The semantic term,
which would have to be large and negative for ``the model does not understand
the error'' to be the explanation, is small and its sign is not even consistent
across our two environments.

That localises the problem in the harness rather than in the model, and it
predicts which remedies act on the right term. Two do. Replacing the verbatim
call with a description the runtime generates from its own error metadata
removes \AbstractRemoved\ of the inversion at no token cost, and banning
previously-failed strings at the decoder acts on the same term. Two do not. A
natural-language prohibition moves the measured quantity slightly the
\emph{wrong} way, as the negative-instruction literature would predict for a
prohibition that must name the string it forbids
\citep{castricato2024pinkelephants,negation2025pinkelephant}. More
surprisingly, deleting the failed attempt and retrying from a clean context,
which is what prior work on context contamination recommends
\citep{yang2026contamination}, is the \emph{worst} harness we measure: it
multiplies the exact repeat rate by \DropRepeatRatio\ without improving task
success. Deleting the failure restores the exact context that produced it, and
a deterministic policy in an identical context emits an identical action.

What survives is narrow enough to be useful:

\begin{quote}
The context after a failure must differ from the context before it, and the
difference must not be the failed action itself.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A measurement, the corrective gain of a failure record, shown negative
across \NumModelsToolshed\ models and two environments, reported alongside a
normalised repeat probability and an exact greedy repeat rate.
(ii) A decomposition into a surface-form and a semantic term, which identifies
the surface form as the cause and predicts which interventions can work.
(iii) An evaluation of three harness interventions in free-running rollouts,
including one that follows from prior work and makes matters worse. Everything
runs on a CPU; code, item sets and raw per-score records are released.

\section{Corrective gain and its decomposition}
\label{sec:ws-gain}

Write $\oplus$ for appending a turn pair to the transcript, so that
$\ctxpre_{t+1} = \ctxpre_t \oplus (a_t, o_t)$. Almost every agent framework
implements that literally, which is the design decision this paper is about.
For a decision point $(\ctxpre, \afail, o^{\times})$ define
\begin{equation}
\Gain(\afail) \;=\; \log \pi(\afail \mid \ctxpre) \;-\;
\log \pi\!\left(\afail \mid \ctxpre \oplus (\afail, o^{\times})\right),
\label{eq:ws-gain}
\end{equation}
where $\log \pi(a \mid c) = \sum_i \log \pi(a_i \mid c, a_{<i})$. Both terms
score \emph{the same string}, so the difference is exactly the change in the
log-odds of writing that action again, and it is invariant to the action's
length and to how the tokeniser splits it. $\Gain > 0$ is the behaviour the
harness is designed to produce. $\Gain < 0$ we call \emph{feedback inversion}.

Let $o^{\varnothing}$ be a \emph{valence-free} observation, which acknowledges
the call but reports neither success nor failure. Then with
$\copyterm = \log \pi(\afail \mid \ctxpre \oplus (\afail, o^{\varnothing}))
- \log \pi(\afail \mid \ctxpre)$ and
$\semterm = \log \pi(\afail \mid \ctxpre \oplus (\afail, o^{\times}))
- \log \pi(\afail \mid \ctxpre \oplus (\afail, o^{\varnothing}))$,
\begin{equation}
-\Gain(\afail) \;=\; \copyterm + \semterm
\label{eq:ws-decomp}
\end{equation}
by construction. $\copyterm$ isolates what merely \emph{having written the
action} does, holding the feedback content neutral; $\semterm$ isolates what
\emph{marking it as failed} adds, holding the surface form fixed.

Equation~\ref{eq:ws-decomp} makes the design space concrete. If $\semterm$
dominates, the model misread the error and the remedy is a better model or a
clearer message. If $\copyterm$ dominates, message engineering cannot reach the
problem, because the damage is done by the presence of the string rather than
by anything said about it, and the remedies must be structural: remove the
surface form from the context, or make it unreachable at decoding time.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:ws-setup}

\paragraph{Environments.}
\emph{ToolShed} is a simulated office workspace with twelve typed tools and six
task templates requiring two to four calls. Failing actions are constructed
from the reference action by \NumToolshedOperators\ perturbation operators
yielding \NumToolshedErrorTypes\ error families, so every model is probed on
byte-identical items and a scaling curve cannot confound model size with item
difficulty. We built it because the decomposition needs, for one fixed action,
an observation reporting failure, one reporting success and one reporting
nothing; standard agent benchmarks are built to score policies and do not
expose counterfactual observations. \emph{CodeRepair} is the sanitised split of
MBPP \citep{austin2021mbpp}, executed in a subprocess against the problem's own
assertions, where failing programs are single-edit mutations and nothing about
the error text is authored by us. It also exercises a regime ToolShed cannot:
actions are whole functions, and a real traceback quotes the offending source
line back at the model.

\paragraph{Models and protocol.}
\NumModels\ instruction-tuned checkpoints spanning
\SmallestModel--\LargestModel\ across \NumFamilies\ families
(\FamilyList), in float32 on a CPU with 17\,GB of RAM, which is what sets the
parameter ceiling. Scoring is teacher-forced and deterministic. Items from one
task share a prompt and a prefix, so every interval is a 95\% cluster bootstrap
over \emph{tasks} ($10{,}000$ resamples); the headline family is Holm-corrected
across models. A model/environment cell with fewer than 30 scored items is
excluded from every table and fit.

The decomposition is the point, and Figure~\ref{fig:ws-decomposition} is the
whole argument in one panel. $\copyterm$ accounts for \CopyShare\ of the
total. $\semterm$ is small, and on ToolShed it is slightly \emph{positive},
plausibly because explaining what went wrong requires naming the tool and the
offending argument again; on CodeRepair it is slightly negative, so a traceback
does push the model away from the program that produced it. We stress the
second case because it fixes the interpretation: these models are not simply
failing to read error messages. Whatever reading happens is swamped, by a
median of \MedianCopySemRatio\ to one and by \MaxCopySemRatio\ to one at the
extreme.

\paragraph{Copying does not simply raise everything.}
The correct action shares most of its tokens with the failed one, so its
probability rises too, by \MeanRepair\ nats. What governs the next move is the
margin, and the margin moves the wrong way: the gold-minus-failed gap shrinks
by \MeanMarginShift\ nats once the failure is recorded. The normalised repeat
probability is computed over a fixed candidate set, so a uniform lift cancels;
it still rises sharply. And the greedy readout is a statement about the argmax,
immune to uniform lifts by construction.

\paragraph{Scale.}
Within a family, where training data is held roughly fixed, the larger
checkpoint has the milder gain, in both environments independently, and the
weakening is driven by $\copyterm$ rather than $\semterm$: larger models copy
less, they do not read the error better. A log-linear fit has slope
\ScalingSlope\ nats per decade ($R^2 = \ScalingRsq$) and reaches zero at
\ZeroCrossing, but we report that number in order to argue against it:
refitting with each model held out moves the crossing from \ZeroCrossingLoMin\
to \ZeroCrossingLoMax, a factor of \ZeroCrossingLoRatio\ decided by which six
checkpoints were affordable. Whether the effect survives at 8B is the first
question a reader with GPUs should ask, and the probe costs a few hundred
forward passes per model to answer.

\section{What moves the effect}
\label{sec:ws-manip}

If the attribution to surface form is right, the effect should respond to
manipulations of the \emph{form} and be largely indifferent to manipulations of
the \emph{message}. Table~\ref{tab:probe-manipulations} reports each variant as
a \emph{paired} change against the standard harness on the same items.

\input{tables/probe_manipulations}

Message content is nearly inert. Shortening the error to a bare category name
changes the quantity by \DTerseCI\ nats and expanding it with the tool
signature and a corrective hint by \DVerboseCI; both intervals contain zero.
Occurrence count is not inert. When the runtime echoes the offending call
inside the error message, as a Python traceback does by default, repetition
becomes \DEchoCI\ nats more likely. Adding information changes nothing; adding
a second copy of the string changes something.

Removing the surface form does the work. Replacing the call with a
runtime-generated description moves the quantity by \DAbstractCI\ nats and
drives the exact greedy repeat rate to zero for every model. Stripping our
wording from that description, so the transcript reads only
\texttt{[attempt 1 failed]}, recovers \DAbstractMinCI\ against
\DAbstractMatchedCI\ for the full abstraction on the same models, so the
benefit is the absence of the string rather than our glosses. A placebo in
which a \emph{different} plausible-but-wrong call is recorded as having failed
recovers \DPlaceboCI\ nats, a large majority but not all of the effect; since
the distractor shares most of its tokens with the scored action, it leaks
copying by construction and bounds the string-specific component from below.

\section{Free-running rollouts}
\label{sec:ws-agent}

The probe holds the trajectory fixed. We also let a model act freely in
ToolShed over \AgentNumTasks\ tasks for up to \AgentMaxSteps\ steps with greedy
decoding, comparing \AgentNumHarnesses\ harnesses that differ only in what the
model sees and how its next token is chosen.

\input{tables/agent_main}

Under the standard harness the agent solves \AgentVerbatimSuccess\ of tasks and
\AgentVerbatimRepeat\ of its failed actions are byte-for-byte repeats. The
prohibition does not reduce repetition (\DeltaInstrRepeat\ points, interval
containing zero), agreeing with the probe. Clearing the context is the worst
case: exact repetition rises to \AgentDropRepeat, a paired difference of
\DeltaDropRepeat\ points, and task success does not improve. Both surface-form
interventions reduce repetition, the decoder ban most cleanly, taking the exact
rate to \AgentVerbBanRepeat\ (\DeltaVerbBanRepeat\ points) and loops from
\AgentVerbatimStuck\ of rollouts to \AgentVerbBanStuck, at no cost in generated
tokens.

Ranked by loop rate the harnesses order exactly as the decomposition predicts:
the two interventions acting on the failed call's surface form at the good end,
the harness that deletes the failure at the bad end, the standard harness
between. We report two things that cut against us. Task success does not follow
that ordering, because at this scale repetition is a substantial but not
majority share of what goes wrong: \ProseShare\ of actions are prose where a
call was expected, which no arrangement of the transcript repairs. And the ban
displaces repetition into paraphrase, which is why we report a canonical repeat
rate alongside the exact one and do not recommend the ban alone.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:ws-limits}

The ladder stops at \LargestModel\ because of a memory limit, not a scientific
choice, and the extrapolated crossing is unstable under leave-one-out, so we
characterise the small-model regime and a direction of travel rather than a
threshold. The rollout study is one model on \AgentNumTasks\ tasks; the
intervals are correspondingly wide. Failing actions are constructed rather than
sampled, which buys byte-identical items across the ladder at the cost of some
realism, and the rollout study, where models generate their own failures, is
where that trade is tested. ToolShed is ours; CodeRepair, whose observations
are written by the Python interpreter, is the check on it and shows the same
pattern.

The claim we make is narrow. Execution feedback is not useless and agent
transcripts should not be thrown away. But the standard way of \emph{delivering}
that feedback hands a small model a strong reason to repeat itself and a weak
reason not to, and a harness can keep the diagnosis while withholding the
reason. For agents built on small models, which is where these effects are
largest and where cost pressure is greatest, that is a change to the harness
and not to the model.
